Why this document exists, and how it differs

The existing reading — 63 sections — was built by accretion: a question was asked, an instrument was applied, a section was written. That produces a thorough document and a badly ordered one. It reaches for Shadbala early, computes vargas at length before the houses they qualify, and arrives at bhava-level judgments last.

This document inverts that. It follows one sequence and does not depart from it:

1 what the bhava represents → 2 the bhava itself: sign, occupants, aspects → 3 the bhava lord → 4 the natural kāraka → 5 the relevant varga → 6 planetary strength → 7 bhava strength → 8 benefic/malefic influence, affliction, yogas → 9 dasha — only now → 10 Ashtakavarga and transit, last

Or, compressed: what is promised (bhava) → who delivers it (lord, kāraka) → how strong (dignity, Shadbala, Bhava Bala, varga) → when (dasha) → how transit modifies it (Ashtakavarga).

No technique is assigned a percentage weight. The bhava and its lord are central; everything after qualifies, corroborates and times. Where a step adds nothing, it is reported as adding nothing rather than padded.

The synthesis, in the sequence's own terms

What is promised — from the bhavas. A chart whose strongest departments are the 12th, the 4th and the 2nd: release, the mother and the home, and wealth held. Whose weakest are the 8th, the 11th and the 6th. And whose self, children and spouse are untouched by any graha at all.

Who delivers it — from the lords and kārakas. Shukra (Ishta 47.49) and Surya (46.88) are the two real deliverers, and both stand in the 8th. Budha, which rules the self and the career, is the one graha in the chart that fails its own minimum. Chandra and Surya are the only exalted, undivided lords, and they hold the 11th and the 12th.

How strong — dignity, Shadbala, Bhava Bala, varga, taken in that order and not before their turn. Six of seven grahas pass Shadbala comfortably; the seventh runs the identity and the profession. The vargas corroborate rather than rescue — the D10 is harsher than the D1, the D7 repeats the D1's silence, and only the D9 adds new information, by putting two malefics in the seventh house of a marriage the rashi chart left untouched.

What this rebuild changed, and what it did not

It changed no computed fact. Every longitude, dignity, strength figure, dasha boundary and bindu count is identical to the existing reading; they come from the same verified ephemeris and the same supplied tables.

It changed the order, and the order changed three things.

  1. The 4th house. Read in sequence it comes out rank 2, clean at step 8, lord in a kendra and in its own 4th in the D4, kāraka exalted at the lowest cost in the chart, and its lord's period running now. The existing reading never assembled those facts in one place, because it never judged the 4th as a bhava — it met the 4th's components scattered across sections on avastha, vargas and the un-asked questions.
  1. The 11th. The existing reading said income was the weak channel, then overturned itself when asked to look at placement alone. The sequence reaches the correct answer in one pass — because step 3 (exalted, undivided lord) comes before step 7 (rank 11 of 12), and the strength number is never allowed to speak first.
  1. The 7th. The rashi seventh is untouched and step 2 returns nothing; the D9 seventh holds Mangal and Ketu. Putting the varga at step 5 rather than early makes this a specific located affliction instead of a general unease.

And it exposed one thing no single-house reading could. All twelve lords stand in three adjacent houses, eight bhavas are empty, and three — self, children, spouse — are untouched entirely. The chart is not twelve departments. It is three rooms administering twelve.
